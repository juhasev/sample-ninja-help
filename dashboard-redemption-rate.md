## Redemption Rate

The redemption rate shows how much of the reward points your panelists earn actually gets paid out to them. It answers a simple question: **are panelists really getting rewarded for their time?**

**Redemption rate = points redeemed ÷ points earned** over the selected period (the last 3, 6, 9 or 12 months).

For example, if panelists earned 1,000,000 points in the last 12 months and redeemed 780,000 points, the redemption rate is **78%**. The remaining 22% is still sitting in panelists' balances.

### What the widget shows

- **Redemption rate** — the rate for the selected period. The arrow shows the change in percentage points compared to the period before it (for example, the 12 months before the last 12 months).
- **Outstanding points** — the total unredeemed balance across all panelists today. This is what you still owe your panel.
- **Earners who redeemed** — of the panelists who earned points during the period, the share who also redeemed during it.
- **Chart** — points earned (blue) and redeemed (green) per month, and the rate line (yellow) calculated over the same number of months as the selected period, ending in each month. The line smooths out single busy or quiet months.

Use the settings menu to change the period or to show selected sub panels only. The download button exports the monthly numbers as a CSV file.

### What counts as earned and redeemed

- **Earned** includes project rewards, profiling rewards, referral rewards and manual balance adjustments. Negative adjustments reduce the earned total.
- Points that are still **pending** approval are not counted as earned until they are approved.
- **Imported balances** (points brought over when panelists were imported into Sample Ninja) are not counted as earned, because they were earned elsewhere and would show up as one huge spike in the month of the import. They are included in the outstanding points.
- **Redeemed** is the points spent on rewards, minus points refunded for rejected or failed redemptions. Refunds are counted in the month the refund happens.

### What it means for panel owners

- **Incentive credibility.** Panelists stay engaged when rewards feel reachable. A low or falling rate often means the payout threshold is too high, the reward catalog is not attractive, or the redemption process is difficult. Panelists who never manage to cash out tend to stop responding.
- **Financial liability.** Outstanding points are money you owe. Track this number together with the rate so the liability does not quietly grow, and so you can budget for redemptions.
- **Early warning of fraud.** A sudden jump in redemptions, especially from new panelists, can indicate fraudulent accounts cashing out. Review the redemption queue when the rate spikes without an obvious reason, such as a reward promotion.
- **Dormant balances.** If many panelists earn but never redeem, consider reminding them of their balance. It is a good reason to reconnect with panelists who are drifting away.

### What it means for sample buyers

- **Respondents are paid.** Buyers and auditors often ask for the redemption rate in panel quality questionnaires as evidence that the incentive scheme is real. Panelists who are actually rewarded are more motivated to complete surveys carefully and honestly.
- **Panel health.** A stable or rising rate suggests an engaged panel that participates for the rewards it is promised. A very low rate can suggest unreachable thresholds or a large share of inactive members, which affects response rates and feasibility.
- **Read it together with other metrics.** The redemption rate is most meaningful next to panel tenure, response rates and churn. There is no single "correct" rate: it depends on your payout threshold, reward catalog and how long panelists have been members.

### Unusual values

> **Over 100%** means panelists redeemed more points than they earned during the period, because they cashed out points earned earlier. This is normal after a reward promotion or when the period is short.

> **Below 0%** means more points were refunded than redeemed during the period, for example when a reward partner failed a batch of orders placed in an earlier month. Refunds are counted in the month they happen, not in the month of the original redemption.

> **No rate (–)** is shown when no points were earned during the period.

> When filtering by sub panel, imported balances that are not linked to a sub panel are left out of the outstanding points.
